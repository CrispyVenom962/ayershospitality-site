60 Seconds with Stephen · #24

The seeping mediocrity in our lives and hotels

By Stephen Ayers · 1 min read

Mediocrity: the quality of being not very good.

It has seeped into our lives so quietly that we barely notice it anymore.

Average service in restaurants. Indifference in hotels. Long lines and blank stares in public offices.

And when someone actually delivers outstanding service, we call it “awesome” - simply because it’s so rare.

That’s the problem.

We’ve lowered our expectations. We expect bland. We tolerate average. We’re pleasantly surprised when something is “not bad.”

Businesses try to fight mediocrity with slogans: “Exceptional service.” “Unique experience.” “The best.”

But saying it doesn’t make it true.

Lowering prices doesn’t fix it either. That just accelerates the decline.

So how do we change it?

It starts with standards. With training. With leadership that refuses to accept average.

And maybe - just maybe - with customers who stop rewarding mediocrity.

Because excellence should not be unusual.

It should be normal.

Wouldn’t that be something.
